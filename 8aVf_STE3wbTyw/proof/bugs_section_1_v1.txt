ISSUE 1 [Runs 2, 3] — Step 1: Budget-to-compute conversion unjustified
  Anchor: Step 1
  Location: "The maximum amount of compute purchasable at time t is C_B(t) = B / k(t)"
  Description: A1 states that hardware cost per FLOP declines, but the proof does not
  explicitly justify the total-cost model "cost of C FLOPs = k(t)*C", does not rule out
  other costs or constraints (e.g., energy, personnel, amortised capital), and does not
  establish k(t) > 0, which is required for the division to be valid.

ISSUE 2 [Runs 2, 3] — Step 1: Best-loss identification unjustified
  Anchor: Step 1
  Location: "the best loss achievable within budget B at time t is L_B(t) = (C_0 / C_B(t))^alpha"
  Description: A1 provides a scaling law for large neural-network models trained with compute
  C. The proof does not establish (a) that the scaling law is the best achievable AI system
  for budget B, (b) that C_B(t) always falls in the "large model" regime required for the
  law to hold, or (c) that no other architecture or paradigm achieves lower loss within the
  same budget. The identification of the scaling-law loss with the globally optimal loss is
  therefore not justified by A1 alone.

ISSUE 3 [Runs 2, 3] — Step 3: Sign of D_L(t) never established
  Anchor: Step 3
  Location: "D_L(t) = L_pre - L_B(t) … the AI system's loss advantage grows over time"
  Description: The proof shows D_L'(t) > 0, i.e. the gap is increasing. It does not
  establish D_L(t) > 0, i.e. that AI actually outperforms the pre-AI baseline. The claim
  of "advantage" requires L_pre > L_B(t), which is neither assumed nor proved. Sub-claim
  (ii) therefore rests on an unverified positivity condition.

ISSUE 4 [Run 2] — Step 3: Sign of D_kappa(t) never established
  Anchor: Step 3
  Location: "D_kappa(t) = kappa_B(t) - kappa_pre … the capability deficit grows at an
  accelerating rate"
  Description: The computation shows D_kappa'(t) > 0 and D_kappa''(t) > 0, but it does not
  establish D_kappa(t) > 0. Without the condition kappa_B(t) > kappa_pre, the quantity is
  not necessarily a "deficit" of the pre-AI system and the label "capability deficit" is
  inconsistent with its definition. Sub-claim (ii) is therefore incomplete.

ISSUE 5 [Runs 2, 3] — Step 5: "Economically dominated" argument formally invalid
  Anchor: Step 5
  Location: "if one adds the economic assumption that, at equal cost, higher kappa strictly
  increases organizational payoff, then whenever kappa_B(t) > kappa_pre, the pre-AI baseline
  is economically dominated by AI adoption at time t"
  Description: The proof's own earlier definition of economic dominance requires payoff to be
  weakly higher in ALL relevant states and strictly higher in at least one. The added
  assumption only supplies a capability-payoff monotonicity condition at equal cost. The proof
  does not (a) specify or bound the relevant state space, (b) account for all non-capability
  payoff factors (cost differentials, risk, transition costs, etc.), or (c) verify the
  required statewise dominance condition. Sub-claim (iii) is therefore not formally
  established.
