ISSUE 4 — Omega lower bound does not imply monotonicity [HIGH]
  Location: "greater integration depth => higher switching cost ... partially
  supported by A4, though only as a lower-bound statement: S(d) >= c d^2"
  The solution correctly notes the implication is "partially" supported, but the
  analysis stops there. S(d) = Omega(d^2) does not imply S is monotone
  increasing: a function can satisfy the lower bound while decreasing between
  specific depths. No monotonicity assumption is stated or justified, yet the
  solution uses the implication "greater depth => higher cost" as if it were
  established.

ISSUE 5 — Countermodel does not fully satisfy A4 [MEDIUM]
  Location: "For each X in S, define d_X = 100 ... S_X(d) = d^2. Then
  S_X(d) = Omega(d^2), so A4 is satisfied."
  Assumption A4 contains two clauses: (a) S(d) = Omega(d^2), and (b) for
  operationally critical systems, deep integration (d >> 1) is the norm.
  The solution checks only clause (a). It does not justify that d_X = 100
  satisfies the informal condition d >> 1, nor that the uniform assignment of
  depth 100 to all four systems satisfies the "norm" clause. A4 may not be
  fully satisfied by this construction.

ISSUE 6 — "Prohibitive" is undefined; falsification of C4(ii) is informal [MEDIUM]
  Location: "This cost is not prohibitive relative to B under any ordinary
  meaning of 'prohibitive.'" and "Thus switching cost is not prohibitive

  The term "prohibitive" appears in C4(ii) but is never formally defined. The

  Without a formal definition or threshold for "prohibitive," the countermodel

  budget comparison. The stronger claim that C4(ii) is false in the constructed
  model remains unestablished.

ISSUE 7 — "Irrevocably AI-dependent" is undefined; falsification of C4(iv) is informal [MEDIUM]
  Location: "Since 10^4 << 10^12, removal is feasible in this model. Hence the
  systems are not irrevocably AI-dependent. This falsifies C4(iv)."
  "Irrevocably AI-dependent" is not formally defined. The argument assumes that
  (a) paying the switching cost is sufficient for successful removal, and (b)
